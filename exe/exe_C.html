Sampling distributions and estimators

NoteExercise A - Solution: mean_median.R

As in Unit I, the daily concentrations of PM2.5 (µg/m³) in a city are described by a gamma distribution with mean \mu = 28 and standard deviation \sigma = 12, that is with shape = \mu^2/\sigma^2 and rate = \mu/\sigma^2.

To estimate \mu from a campaign of n days, the agency could use either the sample mean or the sample median.

  1. Simulate R = 1000 campaigns of n = 20 days. For each campaign, compute both the sample mean and the sample median.

  2. Compare the two estimators through a boxplot, adding a horizontal line at \mu.

  3. Approximate the bias and the mean squared error of the two estimators. Which one is preferable?

  4. The sample median is a biased estimator of \mu. Which quantity of the population does it estimate? Compute it using qgamma.

  5. Repeat points 1 and 3 for a normal population with the same \mu and \sigma. Check that both estimators are now unbiased, and that the standard error of the median is about 1.25\,\sigma/\sqrt{n}.

NoteExit poll in Arizona (AK 3.2) - Solution: exit_poll.R

In an exit poll of n = 1648 voters in the 2020 Arizona Senate race, 51.5\% said they voted for Kelly.

  1. Suppose that exactly 50\% of the population voted for Kelly. Compute the standard error of the sample proportion.

  2. Under the same assumption, simulate R = 5000 exit polls with rbinom, as in Unit I, and draw the histogram of the simulated sample proportions. Compare their standard deviation with the result of point 1.

  3. How often does an exit poll give 51.5\% or more, if \pi = 0.5? Answer both with the simulation and with the central limit theorem. Would you be confident in predicting the winner?

NoteDefective chips - Solution: chips.R

A supplier of electronic chips claims that only 4\% of its chips are defective. A manufacturer tests n = 500 chips, randomly selected from a large shipment, and will return the whole shipment if more than 5\% of them are defective. Assume that the claim of the supplier is true.

  1. Find the mean and the standard error of the sample proportion of defective chips.

  2. Is it reasonable to approximate its distribution with a normal one?

  3. Find the probability that the shipment is returned in three ways: through a simulation with rbinom, through the central limit theorem and exactly, with pbinom. Why do the last two answers differ?

Confidence intervals and tests

NoteCalibration of a CO_2 sensor - Solution: sensor.R

A laboratory checks a CO_2 sensor with a reference gas of 100 ppm. Nine independent measurements (ppm) give

y <- c(100.8, 99.7, 100.2, 101.0, 99.8, 100.3, 100.9, 100.1, 100.8)

The measurements can be assumed normally distributed, and the sensor is well calibrated if their mean \mu is 100 ppm.

  1. State H_0 and H_a to test whether the sensor is miscalibrated.

  2. Compute \bar{y}, s and the observed test statistic. Decide using the critical region, with significance level 0.05 and 0.01.

  3. Compute the P-value. Do the decisions agree?

  4. Check your results with t.test. Find the 95\% and the 99\% confidence intervals for \mu (option conf.level). Do they contain 100? Compare with point 2.

NoteLaws to protect the environment - Solution: environment.R

In a survey, 1223 people out of 1353 said that the government should impose strict laws to make industry do less damage to the environment.

  1. Are the conditions for a confidence interval for the population proportion \pi satisfied?

  2. Compute and interpret the 95\% confidence interval for \pi.

  3. Is there evidence that a majority of the population is in favour? Test H_0: \pi = 0.5 against H_a: \pi > 0.5.

NoteSolar panels - Solution: solar.R

A company claims that a rooftop solar panel installation produces, on average, 18 kWh of electricity per day. The daily production of n = 144 randomly chosen installations has sample mean \bar{y} = 17.60 and sample standard deviation s = 2.40.

  1. Is there evidence that the mean production is lower than claimed? State H_0 and H_a, compute the observed test statistic and decide with significance level 0.01, using the critical region.

  2. Compute the P-value. Does the decision change with significance level 0.05?

NoteStudents dataset (AK 4.14(a)) - Solution: tv.R

The Students data file of Agresti and Kateri (2022), already met in Exercises A, is available at this link. The variable tv is the weekly number of hours spent watching TV.

  1. Import the data and describe the distribution of tv.

  2. Compute the 95\% confidence interval for the mean weekly number of hours spent watching TV, first with the formula of Unit J and then with t.test.

  3. Which assumptions are needed? Are they reasonable here?
